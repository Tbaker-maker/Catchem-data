# EXPERIENCE WORK — deferred past launch, deliberately
The experience agent found seven measurable issues. ONE shipped tonight
(new-since-last-visit — purely additive). The rest are restructuring, and
restructuring the app the night before an audience arrives is how you break
something at the worst possible moment.

## SHIPPED
- **New since your last visit.** A quiet dot on sections that changed since
  the reader was last here, with one line explaining it. localStorage only.

## AFTER LAUNCH — in value order

### 1. Seventeen sections on Today
Every section is a decision the reader makes about whether to keep reading.
Past about six, a page stops being scanned and starts being skipped.
DO NOT DELETE ANYTHING. Rank by how often somebody would act on each and move
the bottom third behind a tap — it stops competing, it does not disappear.

### 2. Thirty-two emoji
A few give warmth. Many make a data product read as a chat window, and they
are the first thing that looks dated in a year. Keep them for section headers
and the ELI5 lollipop; remove from anything carrying a number.

### 3. Four accent colours on one surface
Our own rule is one. Colour carries information; past two or three it becomes
decoration and the eye stops trusting it. Green positive, red negative, mode
accent for everything else, gold as a highlight only.

### 4. Eleven hard-coded colours bypassing tokens
Each is a place the app drifts from the site — the seam a visitor feels
without being able to name. Route through tokens.css.

## THE QUESTIONS ONLY EYES CAN ANSWER
Chat cannot see the app. These are deliberately specific, because "review the
design" is a request nobody can act on:
1. On a 390px phone, how many scrolls before the first number you would act on?
2. Cold cache, mobile data — how long until anything readable appears?
   Over three seconds reads as broken.
3. Do the three Daily Three cards look like siblings, or three designs sharing
   a row?
4. Is the index the first thing the eye lands on, or does something louder win?
5. Squint at Today. Which three things stand out? Are they the three that matter?
6. Does anything look like an ad, a warning, or an error when it is none of those?
7. Is there anywhere a first-time visitor would not know what to do next?
8. Does the app feel like the same company as the site, or a cousin of it?
