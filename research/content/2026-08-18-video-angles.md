## Topic 1 — One Month to the 30th (Sept 16)

- TAKE: "Celebrations 2.0" isn't a conclusion, it's an assumption about print run wearing a costume. The tell: restock cadence in October. Celebrations restocked endlessly for a year. If 30th product cycles through retail without that rhythm, the comparison breaks — possible, not promised. Watch the shelves, not the format.

**TEACHING**

- TAKE: Framework: gem rate × grading cost vs raw price. If a card gems ~1-in-3 and grading costs $80, slab break-even is far above the hype. Run that math on ANY set before sending a stack — it kills more subs than it blesses.
- WHY: Gem-rate math is reusable forever; the all-foil news makes it timely without being news-dependent.

- TAKE: Premium before release = expectation fully priced in; the $200 buyer needs hype to GROW to break even. Possible — Mega Rayquaza is a real draw. But paying tomorrow's price today is risk, not opportunity. Rather be wrong watching than wrong holding.
- WHY: Preorder windows are FOMO at its purest; the Pitch Black timing comparison is the evidence most takes skip.

**TEACHING**
- HOOK: A 10-second test for any preorder premium: ask WHEN the premium showed up.

- TAKE: Travels everywhere — sneakers, tickets, any drop. Post-scarcity premium = information. Pre-release premium = marketing. Ask which one you're looking at before paying over list.
- WHY: One-question frameworks are the most shareable teaching format; applies beyond Pokemon.

## Topic 5 — [BOT-DATA]: Our Tracker Was Wrong for Three Months

**PERSONAL** (lead clip of the batch)
- HOOK: My own price tracker lied to me for three months, and I'm going to show you exactly how.
- SIGNAL: The bot had Journey Together booster boxes at $23.99. Real market ~$290 (now fixed: $285, eBay active listings, Aug 18). Root cause: sorted eBay cheapest-first, took 50, averaged single packs and called them boxes. Every tracker sorting by price has this failure mode built in.
- TAKE: Fixed, validated against live listings, flagged bad history as contaminated instead of deleting — the commits are public. In a hobby where graders edit silently, showing your own bug IS the product. Trust isn't never being wrong; it's the ten minutes after you find out.
- WHY: Nobody else can make this clip; proprietary vulnerability markets the platform better than accuracy claims.

**TEACHING**
- HOOK: How a price tracker can be confidently wrong by 1,100% — and how to spot it in any data you're shown.
- SIGNAL: Three parts: a query matching too much ("booster box" also matches packs/bundles), a sort biasing the sample (cheapest-first), an average laundering it into one clean wrong number. $23.99, total confidence.
- TAKE: Portable lesson: never trust a price without asking what listings went INTO it. Median of what, sorted how, filtered by whom? A dashboard that can't answer is doing vibes with decimals. Protects you from bad trackers, bad comps, bad sold-listing screenshots.
- WHY: Turns an internal bug into universal data literacy.

**CONTRARIAN**
- HOOK: Everyone wants more data in this hobby. Wrong — most of you need less data and better questions.
- SIGNAL: Our tracker published a wrong number for three months and nothing looked broken — smooth chart, precise number. Precision and accuracy are different things; a bad pipeline manufactures precision effortlessly.
- TAKE: The fix wasn't more data — it was reading ten actual eBay titles behind the number. Ten listings beat ninety days of automated history. Before adding another tracker, ask if you've ever checked what's under the one you use.
- WHY: "More data" is the unexamined consensus of the niche — and we argue against our own product category with receipts, the most disarming position available.
