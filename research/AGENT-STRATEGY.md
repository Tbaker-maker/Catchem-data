# AGENT STRATEGY — point them at ourselves, not at output
*Tyler, Aug 23: "how can we leverage agents more, based on everything we
stand by. How can they make us stronger, make the community tighter."*

## THE THESIS
Everyone uses agents to PRODUCE MORE — more posts, more summaries, more
listings. Volume is the one thing in this market that is already free and
already worthless. Our moat is trust and culture, and neither compounds
by producing more of anything.

So: point agents AT OURSELVES. An agent that attacks our own claims every
morning is worth more than ten that write copy, because it makes the one
asset a competitor cannot copy — a public record of being right, and of
correcting fast when we are not — compound without a human remembering to
do it.

Second thesis, for the community: agents should make members feel SEEN,
not served. An agent that answers questions is a help desk. An agent that
notices what a specific person cares about, in public, in front of their
peers, is a community.

## TIER 1 — AGENTS THAT ATTACK US (nobody does this)

### 1. THE FALSIFIER
Every house thesis (RT-1…RT-6) ships with the condition that would prove
it wrong. Nobody ever checks them on a schedule. This agent does, daily:
walks each thesis, tests its falsifier against today's data, and reports
SURVIVED / TRIPPED / INSUFFICIENT DATA.
When one trips, it drafts the public amendment.
WHY IT IS UNEXPLORED: every analyst publishes predictions; almost none
publish the conditions under which they would abandon them, and none
automate the check. This turns our most distinctive habit into a daily
artifact — "we tried to disprove ourselves today, here is the scoreboard."
COST: cheap. The theses and falsifiers already exist in the repo.

### 2. THE NEWCOMER
Reads every published surface as someone who has never bought a card, and
flags each sentence where they would stop understanding. Our jargon linter
matches patterns; this one actually reads. It is the Cliff Rule with
comprehension instead of regex.
Output: a weekly "where a beginner falls off" list.
WHY IT MATTERS: the Cliff Rule says confusion loses readers silently.
A pattern matcher cannot feel confusion. An agent asked to read as a
newcomer can.

### 3. THE RED TEAM
Before a READ-chipped claim publishes, an agent argues the strongest
opposite case from the same data. If the counter-argument is good, we
publish it alongside — "here is the other read." If it is weak, the claim
ships stronger for having survived.
WHY: it is the Referee Doctrine turned inward. We already refuse to take
sides between buyers and vendors; this refuses to take sides with our own
first instinct.

### 4. THE CORRECTION HUNTER
Re-checks numbers we published days or weeks ago against what they turned
out to be, and files anything that drifted materially to the corrections
page — without waiting for a human to notice.
WHY: we already publish our misses. This makes the record complete rather
than dependent on someone remembering.

## TIER 2 — AGENTS THAT TIGHTEN THE COMMUNITY

### 5. THE SHOW FLOOR NETWORK (the biggest unclaimed dataset)
Card-show prices exist nowhere. No index, no history, no comps — it is the
largest liquid Pokémon market with zero public data.
Members photograph a booth price tag; an agent reads it, returns the Deal
Zone instantly in-channel, and — with consent — records the observation.
Do that across a season and we hold the only show-floor price dataset in
the hobby, built by the community, owned with them rather than scraped
from them.
WHY IT IS INSANE AND NOBODY HAS DONE IT: everyone tries to out-scrape eBay.
The uncontested data is the market that was never digitised at all.
CONSTRAINTS THAT MAKE IT OURS: the Referee Doctrine means vendors must
benefit too — a vendor whose prices are competitive gets shown as such —
and consent is explicit, never covert. We are not building a snitch line.

### 6. THE MEMBER AGENT (public, not private)
Each member's watchlist gets an agent that speaks IN THE CHANNEL when one
of their products moves — "morning @X, the Evolving Skies box you starred
is down 4%." Not a DM. Public.
WHY PUBLIC IS THE WHOLE IDEA: a DM is a notification. A public mention is
a moment where the room learns what someone cares about, and other members
answer before we do. It manufactures the exact behaviour a community needs
and cannot be told to perform.

### 7. THE QUESTION LISTENER
Reads the Discord for what members actually ask that our instruments do
not answer — "three people asked about Japanese sealed this week and we
track none of it" — and files it as product input.
WHY: it makes the community's curiosity the roadmap, rather than our
guesses about it. The strongest version publishes the list, so members
watch their own questions turn into features.

### 8. THE WELCOME
A new member arrives; the agent asks two questions, sets their mode, hands
them their first berry, and introduces them BY NAME to two members who
care about the same era. Not a rules dump — a hand on the shoulder.

## THE LAWS THAT GOVERN ALL OF THEM
1. **No-Guessing applies to agents absolutely.** An agent that cannot
   verify something says so and routes it. Agents are the easiest place
   for a confident fabrication to enter, because they produce fluent text
   by default.
2. **Every agent output carries its chip.** VERIFIED or READ, same as ours.
3. **Agents draft; they never publish unreviewed to a public surface.**
   The drafts-for-human pattern, exactly as with Reply Radar.
4. **No agent may impersonate a person.** Ours are labelled as what they
   are, always. Deception would cost the only thing we sell.
5. **Community agents ask consent before recording anything a member says
   or photographs.** Covert collection is not a growth tactic, it is a
   betrayal with a delay.
6. **An agent that only produces volume gets switched off.** Volume is
   free and worthless; if an agent is not making us more accountable or
   the community tighter, it is noise with a salary.

## WHERE TO START
The Falsifier. It is cheap, it runs on data we already hold, it produces
a daily artifact nobody else in this hobby can produce, and it is the
purest expression of what this company already is: a thing that tries to
prove itself wrong before anyone else gets the chance.
