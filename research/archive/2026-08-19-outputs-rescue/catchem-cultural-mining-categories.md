**Status:** Source material for archetype rewrite. Not the rewrite itself.
**Created:** Apr 22, 2026 (late night, after Tyler flagged the 84 archetypes as culturally generic)
**Why this exists:** Tyler: "WE NEED CULTURE OR WE'LL FAIL."

---

## The problem we're solving

The original 84 archetypes (`catchem-card-art-prompts.md`) describe generically emotional creatures — anxious ferns, melted ice cream, friendly ghosts. They're fine art but they don't SPEAK Pokemon-collector culture. They could work for any indie feelings product.

Catch'em's moat isn't the Trading UI, the scoring system, or the Feels packs. **The moat is cultural authenticity.** Collectrics can copy mechanics. They cannot copy the actual voice of the hobby, because that has to be felt, not designed.

The rewrite needs archetypes that make a Pokemon collector think "holy shit, they know." Not "aw, that's cute."

---

## 10 categories to mine for authentic material

### 1. Iconic real Pokemon (reframed in Catch'em voice)

Not generic "small fire dragon." Actual Charizard. Actual Moonbreon. But written as Catch'em characters with Catch'em feelings.

Examples to develop:
- **The Charizard Tax** — the Pokemon who knows he's the reason everyone's here
- **The Moonbreon Believer** — already have this one, it works
- **Mewtwo, Tired** — the legendary who's been the grail for 25 years and is ready to retire
- **Pikachu, Overrated Actually** — the opinion every serious collector has held at least once
- **The Gyarados Who Remembers Being Magikarp**
- **Lugia (Silver Tempest Alt)** — the grail that almost nobody got
- **Giratina V Alt Art** — the one that makes people scream
- **Umbreon VMAX Alt Art** — Moonbreon's real name, the card that broke the hobby

### 2. Pokemon's source biology (deep-cut references)

Real animals/creatures that inspired Pokemon designs. Writing archetypes that acknowledge the real biology delights the fans who know.

Source material:
- **Mudkip → Axolotl** (the most obvious one, but done right)
- **Drowzee → Malayan tapir** (the "dream eater" mythology + tapirs eating dreams in Asian folklore)
- **Shellos → Sea slug / nudibranch** (color morph based on region = real biology)
- **Cubone → Baby kangaroo wearing its dead mother's skull** (the dark one)
- **Magnemite → Literal screws and magnets** (the industrial one)
- **Grimer → Pollution, specifically Japan's 1970s mercury crisis**
- **Voltorb → A Poké Ball that achieved consciousness** (existential)
- **Jynx → Yamamba, Japanese mountain witch yokai** (the controversial one)
- **Drampa → Grandpa Pokemon based on wise elder mythology**

Write archetypes that reference the REAL inspiration, not just the Pokemon. Rewards knowledge.

When we rewrite the 84 archetypes, we pull FROM these categories. Not all archetypes need to come from category #1. A healthy distribution might be:

- ~20 archetypes from iconic Pokemon (category 1)

- ~15 from collector rituals (category 3) — the "yes that's me" reaction
- ~8 from investor culture (category 4)

## The voice stays Catch'em

Cultural specificity doesn't mean abandoning Catch'em's soft-absurd-with-anxiety voice. It means running that voice OVER authentic collector material.

**Wrong:**
> "The PSA Sub Waiter — a person who waits for PSA grades."

**Right:**
> "i sent them in february. i check every tuesday. the website still says 'received.' i am fine."

Catch'em voice (lowercase vulnerability, specific timeframes, the quiet "i am fine" that means they're not fine) + real collector experience (PSA submission waits) = cultural specificity.

---

## Non-negotiable principles for the rewrite

1. **Every archetype must pass the "a collector would feel seen" test.** If a random Pokemon collector read it and didn't recognize their own life, it's wrong.

2. **Deep cuts earn loyalty.** The axolotl → Mudkip reference rewards the fan who knows. They become evangelists.

3. **Cultural specificity doesn't mean insider gatekeeping.** Newbies should feel invited to learn the vocabulary, not excluded. Lore > trivia.

4. **The archetypes are the brand, not the art style.** If the art is gorgeous but the writing is generic, we lose. If the art is mediocre but the writing nails the culture, we win.

5. **Voice stays soft-absurd-with-anxiety.** We're not pivoting to bro-collector energy. We're adding cultural specificity to Catch'em's existing voice.
