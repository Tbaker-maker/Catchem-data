## Key thesis points (his opinion, explicitly framed)

### 1. Bull run is cooling at the edges

**The arc he's tracking:**
- Stella Crown: first unprecedented pre-order levels (Bulbasaur/Squirtle chase)
- Surging Sparks: pushed envelope further
- Prismatic Evolutions → Destined Rivals → Fantasmal Flames → Ascended Heroes: continued bull run
- **Perfect Order: first set to break the streak** — "people that are in the market only for quick dollars" start exiting
- Chaos Rising: prediction is "maybe slightly better than Perfect Order, but not much"
- Dark Cry base (coming after Chaos Rising): unknown

**His take:** Cooling is actually healthy if you're a long-term holder. And 30th anniversary alone could reignite everything (like 151 did mid-Scarlet/Violet when the market was quiet).

**This confirms Alex/Nostalgianomics' structural thesis from different angle.** Two independent creators reading the surrounding-set weakness as a meaningful signal.

### 2. 151 is in active correction

Hard numbers (his claims, should cross-check with Catchem-data):
- **Total set value:** peaked ~$25,000, now ~$22,250 (**10% pullback in ~1 month**)
- **Top chase cards:** ~10% down
- **Example: Charmander $150 → $105** (~30% down)
- But: **50-60% appreciation over the prior 6 months**

His framing: "if something shoots up so rapidly, there's bound to be a correction." Not catastrophic, just normal market behavior.

**Signals newsletter angle:** The 151 retraction is happening AT THE SAME TIME as Ascended Heroes mania. Two different things going on in Pokemon right now. Signals can track both with data.

### 3. He publicly reversed his famous ETB thesis

**Old position** (the 100k-view video): Don't invest in main-set ETBs. Reasoning: they're printed too heavily to match Sun/Moon-era ROI.

**New position:** Some Sword/Shield ETBs now represent good value, particularly ones with strong artwork. Specifically called out:
- **Fusion Strike ETB** (Mew artwork, bright colors — compared to Celestial Storm Rayquaza)
- **Lost Origin ETB**
- **Astral Radiance ETB**

**Key insight for Catch'em voice:** he articulates WHY ETBs have different end-users than BBs. Four buyer types:
1. Streamers/ripen-shippers/YouTubers (content fuel)
2. Collectors (display, sealed ETB collections are real)
3. Investors (buy-and-hold)
4. Secondary investors (flip to next investor)

This 4-buyer taxonomy is genuinely useful framework. Not just "collectors buy stuff" — different segments buy for different reasons.

### 4. Sealed cases reality check

Warning against assuming everything goes up:
- **Perfect Order case example:** ~AU$2,000 now. Could sit at that level for 5 years, move to $2,200 = terrible ROI.
- Better: put same capital into **Destined Rivals booster boxes** (proven winners).

**His rule of thumb:** don't park money in subpar sets just because "eventually everything goes up." Even Darkness & Blaze took years to move.

### 5. The era debate: sword/shield vs scarlet/violet

His answer: **doesn't matter.** What matters is the SET.

- **Chase cards + artwork + pull difficulty = price**
- **Evolving Skies vs Darkness & Blaze is the perfect comparison** — both Sword/Shield, radically different trajectories

**His long-term winners across eras:**
- Sword/Shield: Evolving Skies, Fusion Strike, Lost Origin
- Scarlet/Violet: Destined Rivals, Surging Sparks (underdog), Paldea Evolved (Magikarp card)

### 6. Storage advice (simple but canonical)

- Keep DRY — moisture is the enemy
- Elevated (off floors)
- Plastic sealed containers for bug protection
- Silica gel packets if concerned about humidity
- Personal anecdote: had Dragon Ball Z sealed in a 1950s house for years, opened them eventually (regrets)

**For Catch'em Signals Grader pillar:** storage tips rotation — evergreen content that never goes out of date.

### 7. 30th Anniversary wishlist (his recipe)

Scored 25th Anniversary honestly: "lucky to scrape a 5 or 6 out of 10."

**What 30th needs to succeed:**
- Main set MUCH bigger (25th's was too small)
- Unique chase cards in the main set
- Classic Collection style subset continuation
- Nicer holo pattern (25th's was "meh")
- **Unique reverse holo pattern** specific to 30th (his big idea)
- Maybe fireworks-style reverse holo energy
- **Gold baby starters** (Charmander, Squirtle, Bulbasaur) + Pikachu
- Smaller 30th logo on cards (25th's was too big, took up 1/4 of card)
- Tiered chase: regular, SIRs, golds

**If it hits:** reignites whole market like 151 did.
**If it misses:** cooling could continue longer.

### 8. Time in market > timing the market

His mantra: **"Fast money equals slow problems."**

- 2-year hold: timing matters a lot
- 5-10 year hold: $220 vs $260 entry point doesn't matter
- Don't quit your job to flip Pokemon
- Don't expect every set to be a hit

**For Catch'em voice:** this is a perfect philosophical anchor. The Signals newsletter can be the "patient" voice in a space dominated by hype cycles.

### 9. New production plant (his take)

The basic math: Pokemon Company is leaving money on the table when product sells out at retail and scalpers capture the spread. New facility = supply/demand rebalancing.

**His prediction:** new production won't flood English market specifically — will be distributed across languages (Japanese, Korean, Chinese, German, Italian all growing). So Ascended Heroes-style instant sellouts become less common, but vintage/low-print scarcity isn't affected.

**Signals angle:** global Pokemon TCG demand is a story most English-only creators don't tell. Could be a Flipper-pillar piece.
