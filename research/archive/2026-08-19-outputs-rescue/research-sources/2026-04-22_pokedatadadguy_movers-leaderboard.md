## Executive summary

PokeDataDadGuy launches a **market-movers leaderboard** on Collectrics that ranks every card by supply saturation shift (tightening/loosening) rather than price movement. To stress-test the system, he buys **18 copies of Nacli** (the Pokemon with the lowest character premium rank in his database — literally the least popular character). The leaderboard detects the activity, registering demand pressure increase and supply saturation decrease for Nacli. Importantly, even with 18 purchased copies, Nacli doesn't crack the top of his leaderboard — other cards are moving faster naturally. He uses this to validate the system and demonstrate organic movement on Riolu SIR and Dachsbun. He also shares his **monetization philosophy**: free-first, small one-time purchases for features (dark mode, expansion packs for card coverage), explicitly NOT subscription-based.

**Why this video matters strategically:** This is the second shipped feature in under a month. Iteration velocity is clearly high. Monetization strategy is now public. He's building something real, fast, and openly.

## Key insights

### The leaderboard concept

- Ranks cards by **supply index shift** (his term for supply saturation shift)
- Default sort: tightening (supply being absorbed — often precedes price moves)
- Can also sort by loosening
- Answers the question "what is actually moving in the market right now?"
- Critical positioning quote: **"This isn't like some other sites where you just see, oh, here's the biggest movers and it's price movers. Price tells you what's already happened. Real market movement is the exchange of goods."**

### The Nacli experiment — what we can learn from it

He identified Nacli as having the lowest character premium rank in his database (below Dachsbun, which he previously mocked on-video). He bought 18 copies.

**Result:** The tool detected the activity. Demand pressure moved from ~1% to elevated; supply saturation shifted to 0.6 (tightening).

**But here's the interesting finding:** Even with 18 copies purchased in one day, Nacli didn't hit the top of the leaderboard. Why?
- High pre-existing active listing count (418)
- 18 purchases is meaningful but not system-shocking at that base
- Other cards were moving faster organically

**What this reveals:**
- The leaderboard is resistant to small-scale manipulation (good)
- Organic market activity can exceed deliberate manipulation for low-demand cards (interesting)
- Demand pressure scales with base listing count (important — same 20 sales is 5% of 400 listings vs 40% of 50 listings)

### Example cards from the leaderboard

**Riolu SIR (Shiny Ultra Rare):**
- Tightening supply to nearly zero listings
- Direct correlation with price increase
- Single-day spike: ~19 copies sold
- Interpretation: someone tried to buy out the card, succeeded in creating scarcity

**Dachsbun:**
- Low demand → suddenly high demand
- His earlier mockery of the card seems to have driven organic buying
- Now appearing on leaderboard
- ~28 listings remaining

**General observation:** Small supply + meaningful demand = rapid leaderboard movement. Large supply + meaningful demand = less leaderboard movement but price pressure still builds.

### The AI-coding acknowledgment (repeated)

He again mentions AI-assisted front-end coding is how he's moving this fast. His backend data infrastructure is hand-built (his professional background). This combination is increasingly common — domain expert + AI coding assistant = rapid product iteration.

## Implications for Catch'em

### 1. Iteration velocity is now visible

In ~2 weeks: pricing model video → market dynamics tool launch → movers leaderboard launch → stated monetization roadmap. That's fast. Not Silicon Valley fast, but solo-builder-with-AI-tools fast.

**What this means for Catch'em:**
- Expect Collectrics to add 1-2 new features every 2-4 weeks
- Catch'em's "design everything perfectly first" approach will fall behind in feature count
- Need to ship something public soon to establish presence

### 2. His monetization strategy creates an opportunity for Catch'em

**He explicitly rejects subscription** and plans free-first with small one-time purchases. This is great for him but caps his revenue ceiling naturally.

**Catch'em can choose differently** — not because subscription is better universally, but because:
- Catch'em's scope (newsletter + app + community + gamification) supports broader monetization
- Affiliate partnerships with Pokemon Center / TCGPlayer / authentication services
- Premium tier for advanced features doesn't hurt Collectrics-alternative users (they'll use free Collectrics for basic metrics; Catch'em-premium users pay for the full platform)
- Creator partnerships, sponsored Signals issues, community monetization

**Strategic implication:** Catch'em is not forced to choose "free or subscription." It's a platform, not a tool. It can have both free and premium layers without contradicting positioning.

### 3. The Nacli experiment is a teachable moment

His willingness to stress-test his own system on video is good content. Catch'em could do equivalent "methodology transparency" content:
- "We ran our intrinsic value model on every Charizard ex ever printed — here's what it said"
- "We tested the chase-leads-box thesis on our data — here's what we found"
- "We bought 20 copies of [common card] to test our demand detection"

These are both credibility-building content and differentiation — showing the math, showing the rigor, showing the willingness to be wrong publicly.

### 4. The "leading indicators not price" framing is important

This is the second video in a row where he's hammered on this: price is lagging, volume/flow is leading. Catch'em should lean in:

- Newsletter Signals should prioritize leading indicators over price history
- App dashboards should show velocity/flow metrics, not just price charts
- Editorial voice should consistently frame "here's what's about to happen" rather than "here's what happened"

Catch'em should use its own phrasing — don't quote him — but the insight is correct and worth adopting.

### 5. The market-movers leaderboard concept

His leaderboard ranks by supply dynamics, not price movement. Catch'em should absolutely build something like this eventually — but it should be **conceptually distinct**. Ideas:

- **"Catch'em Pulse"** — composite leading-indicator rank (volume + supply + demand delta)
- **"Heat Map"** — multi-dimensional view (hot/cold) rather than single-metric ranking
- **Three-pillar leaderboards** — "What's moving for Collectors" vs "What's moving for Flippers" vs "What's moving for Graders" (distinct from his one-size-fits-all view)

Catch'em's three-pillar segmentation is a real differentiator here. He has one leaderboard for everyone. Catch'em can have three leaderboards for three user types.

## Counter-observations / where Catch'em can go deeper

1. **His leaderboard has no time window customization.** Can't ask "biggest movers this week" vs "this month." Catch'em can add this.

2. **His leaderboard mixes all card rarities together.** A shiny ultra rare tightening 30% is framed the same as a common tightening 30%. Catch'em can filter by rarity tier.

3. **No saved/favorited cards.** You can't set up a watchlist on his site (yet — might come). Catch'em's watchlist is a clear utility differentiator.

4. **Nacli experiment has self-congratulation energy.** The fact that 18 purchases didn't break the leaderboard is framed positively — "robust!" But it also means the system requires large activity to register. For long-tail cards with low base listing counts, small volume changes could still manipulate rankings. Catch'em should quantify the sensitivity of its own metrics.

5. **Coverage is "modern Pokemon sets" but scope unclear.** He hasn't published a list of what cards are covered. Catch'em can be explicit: here are the 130 sets we index, here's what's in scope, here's what's not.
