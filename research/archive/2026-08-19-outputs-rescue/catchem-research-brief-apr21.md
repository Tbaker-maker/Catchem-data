## Section 2: The rotation that just happened (April 10, 2026)

### What rotated out (G regulation mark)
All G-mark cards are no longer Standard legal. Affected sets:
- Scarlet & Violet (base)
- Paldea Evolved
- Obsidian Flames
- Paradox Rift
- 151 (fully)
- Paldean Fates (partial — G reprints only)

### What remains Standard legal
- H-mark: Temporal Forces, Twilight Masquerade, Shrouded Fable (and partial in Paldean Fates)
- I-mark: Stellar Crown, Surging Sparks, Prismatic Evolutions, Journey Together, Destined Rivals, Black Bolt & White Flare
- J-mark: Mega Evolution era (Ascended Heroes was first J-mark set via Start Deck 100 cards, Perfect Order was first main set in J-era)

### Mechanics note
Card legality is based on the **regulation mark on the card**, not the set. A Rare Candy from Sun & Moon can still be used because a reprint exists with an I or J mark. Same card name, different legality based on which print you're using.

### Pokemon TCG Live rotation
TCG Live rotated March 26, 2026 — two weeks before in-person rotation — to let players test the new format.

### Next rotation
April 2027 will rotate out H-mark cards (Temporal Forces, Twilight Masquerade, Shrouded Fable, Paldean Fates). That's the 12-month watch window.

## Section 3: The reprint history (this is REAL data collectors care about)

### TPCi's 2025 reprint commitments (public record)
TPCi made **three separate public statements** in 2025 acknowledging supply issues and committing to reprints:
- **January 15, 2025** — Post-Prismatic Evolutions release. Statement: "actively working to print more of the impacted Pokemon TCG products as quickly as possible and at maximum capacity."
- **March 2025** — Second statement, around Journey Together release.
- **March 27, 2025** — Third statement before Journey Together launch.

### Confirmed reprint events
- **Prismatic Evolutions** — Reprinted aggressively throughout 2025 into 2026. First restocks appeared through GameStop in May 2025 (ETB + Tech Sticker Collection May 10, Super Premium Collection later in May). Reprint wave was heavy enough to suppress long-term chase card appreciation.
- **151** — Reprinted heavily across 2024-2025 including Costco bundles (January 2025 Canada, then US). Despite heavy reprints, key cards (Charizard ex SIR) continued appreciating because demand outpaced even aggressive supply response.
- **GameStop restocks (May 2025)** — Confirmed restocks for 151 (Zapdos ex Collection May 24) and Prismatic Evolutions (ETB, Tech Sticker, Super Premium, Binder Collection).

### The scalper dynamic
TPCi's statements explicitly acknowledge that scalping drives shortages. Pokemon Center has had to suspend in-store sales in some regions (Singapore specifically) due to fighting over product. Bot-driven scalping remains an ongoing problem that TPCi's reprint policy is partly designed to counter.

### The "how long does a reprint take" pattern
Historical TPCi pattern: **6-9 months from announcement to shelves.** This is a useful signal — when TPCi announces a reprint, don't expect it soon.

## Section 4: The grading landscape (facts only, no speculation)

### PSA Grading Scandal (December 2025)
- A Pokemon collector submitted ~30 identical modern cards.
- Most returned as PSA 9.
- Collector accepted buyback offers at PSA 9 values (~$35 each).
- 11 of those same cert numbers were then silently updated to PSA 10 without notification.
- Community outrage peaked December 2025 through January 2026.
- Reported 15% volume increase at alternative graders (Beckett, SGC) following scandal.
- PSA still holds dominant market share despite backlash.
- Story broken by community investigation, covered by Yahoo Sports and multiple outlets.
- PSA has not conclusively addressed the allegations publicly as of this writing.

### CGC Prototype Scandal (January-February 2025)
- CGC had authenticated hundreds of Pokemon prototype/playtest cards, some signed by Takumi Akabane (a real Pokemon TCG developer).
- Buyers using forensic equipment discovered yellow-dot printer metadata showing cards were printed in 2024, not vintage as claimed.
- Some cards sold at auction for over $204,000.
- CGC eventually recalled 1,500+ cards amid investigation.
- Most of the alleged counterfeits were "low quality" prototypes — the highest-value signed ones are still under debate.
- Real exploit: color laser printers all embed near-invisible yellow dot patterns encoding printer serial number, time, and date. This was the "tell" that broke the case.

### Other 2025-2026 grading incidents
- **January 2026** — Anthony Curcio convicted of wire fraud for selling Pokemon and sports cards with fake PSA grading labels, including 1999 Venusaur and Charizard worth approximately $2 million.
- Ongoing alteration scandals — Multiple documented cases of card surfaces being altered (bleaching, recoloring, edge restoration) and slipping past graders.

### Grading company landscape as of April 2026
- **PSA** — ~72% market share, dominant despite Dec 2025 scandal. Graded 19M+ cards in 2025. Pokemon dominated submissions (97 of top 100 cards by volume).
- **CGC** — ~17% market share, #2 grader. Grew fast on Pokemon-specific growth. Recovering from prototype scandal.
- **Beckett (BGS)** — Fallen to #4, relies heavily on Pokemon. Offers BGS Black Label (perfect 10s across 4 subgrades, rare and highly valued).
- **SGC** — Niche, vintage-focused. Low gem rate (21%) because of vintage skew.
- **TAG** — AI-based grading. $12-15 per card. Gaining acceptance, growing fast.
- **ARS** — Newer entrant, less data.

### 2025 Pokemon grading stats (real numbers)
- 97 of the top 100 most-submitted cards in 2025 were Pokemon.
- Pikachu was the most-submitted subject (345,000+ graded examples).
- "Pikachu with Grey Felt Hat" (Van Gogh Museum 2023) became the most-graded Pokemon card of all time with 84,000+ examples.
- Iono's Wattrel Battle Partners Promo (Japanese) was most-submitted individual card in 2025 with 45,600+ examples.
- TCG gem rate ~50% across all graders, sports cards ~34%.
- PSA gem rate: 43% overall, 50% for TCG.
- Beckett gem rate: 60%.
- CGC gem rate: 55%.
