## 1. Strategy

### What we're doing
Reframing the audience without abandoning them. The pivot story isn't "I quit NFTs for Pokemon" — it's "I followed the signal." That's a strategic relabel: the same pattern-recognition skills that worked in NFT markets (supply, demand, hype cycles, distribution) apply to Pokemon TCG sealed and singles. We're not switching skill sets. We're applying them to a market that's bigger, older, and more cultural.

### Voice rules
- **Observational, not prescriptive** — "possible breakout imminent" not "ape now"
- **Specific over abstract** — name SKUs, prices, dates, percentages
- **Wyckoff phases for credibility** — uses real market vocabulary, not hype words
- **Acknowledge the past** — don't pretend the NFT era didn't happen
- **Bridge with humility** — Pokemon is not crypto, don't pretend you've always been here

### What we're NOT doing
- Not deleting old NFT posts (history is part of the story)
- Not subtweeting NFT crowd / dunking on crypto
- Not pretending to be a 20-year Pokemon collector
- Not hard-selling Catch'em on day 1
- Not posting more than 3-4x/day (audience will churn from spam)

## 3. Daily engagement strategy

### Posting cadence
- **1 anchor post per day** (the one above)
- **0-2 supporting posts** (replies, quote-tweets, takes)
- **Max 4 posts/day total** — more than that, audience tunes out

### Whose conversations to enter
- **PokeOz, Alex/Nostalgianomics, Collectrics founder** — engage thoughtfully, never combative
- **Pop report announcements (PSA, CGC, ACE)** — quick takes on what it means
- **Pokemon Company news** — set announcements, retailer changes, tournament news
- **Anyone discussing Wyckoff or market cycles in TCG** — rare, but you become the obvious authority

### What to avoid
- Don't dunk on NFT crowd publicly
- Don't engage with hype-merchants trying to attach themselves
- Don't argue about prices in the replies
- Don't promise specific price predictions

### Reply tone
- Short, specific, observational
- Acknowledge counter-arguments instead of dismissing
- Use data when challenged ("Here's what eBay supply looks like for that SKU last 4 weeks...")

## 6. Risks and mitigations

### Risk: Audience reacts badly to NFT acknowledgment
**Mitigation:** Don't apologize for the past. Reframe it. The honest framing ("I followed the signal") works better than "I was wrong about NFTs."

### Risk: Pokemon-native audience sees you as a tourist
**Mitigation:** Days 4-7 educational posts show you actually understand the market. Don't pretend to be a 20-year collector. Be a market reader who's looking at this asset class now.

### Risk: Crypto-native vocabulary alienates Pokemon audience
**Mitigation:** Use Wyckoff terms (Markup, Distribution, Markdown, Accumulation) — they're cross-asset. Avoid "ape," "moon," "wagmi" — those are crypto-coded.

### Risk: Posts blur together / audience fatigue
**Mitigation:** Vary post types. Days 1-3 personal narrative. Days 4-5 educational. Days 6-8 product reveal. Days 9-11 thesis/proof. Days 12-14 community/launch. Different texture each day.

### Risk: Launch day has bot bugs / newsletter delays
**Mitigation:** Have a contingency post ready: "Hitting some last-minute polish. Launch shifts 24 hours. Won't ship broken." Audience respects "we delayed for quality" more than "everything is fine" lies.
